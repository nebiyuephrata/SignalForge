# Evening Call Summary — Day 3

In the evening call, Ephrata's main feedback on the explainer she received for Nureye Nigus's question was that the answer landed best once the asymmetry was made very explicit: SimPO-style pairwise training can let a small judge learn **rejection boundaries** faster than **acceptance calibration**, because ranking loss only needs separability and the rejected side of the data often contains cheaper, more repeated cues. The most useful refinement was making harder negatives feel like a change to the reward geometry rather than just "more difficult examples," especially when those negatives stay lexically compliant but differ in real business value. On Ephrata's own side, the feedback emphasized that her Day 3 question is strongest when it stays tightly centered on that same mechanism in her Week 11 critic: not generic shortcut learning, but why chosen/rejected pair construction and small-model capacity make lexical compliance easier to internalize than commercially useful judgment, and why that is exactly what the next preference-data redesign has to address.
